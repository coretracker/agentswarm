The Sun orbits the [Galactic Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galactic_Center) at a distance of 24,000 to 28,000 [light-years](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light-year). Its [mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arithmetic_mean) distance from Earth is about 1.496×108 kilometres or about 8 [light-minutes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light-minute). The distance between the Sun and the Earth was used to define a unit of length called the [astronomical unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomical_unit) (au), now defined to be 149.5978707×106 kilometres. It is the [largest and most massive object in the Solar System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Solar_System_objects_by_size); [its diameter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_radius) is about 1,391,400 km (864,600 mi), around 109 times that of Earth. [The Sun's mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sun%27s_mass) is around 330,000 times that of Earth, making up about 99.86% of the total mass of the Solar System. The mass of the Sun's surface layer, its [photosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photosphere), consists mostly of [hydrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen) (~73%) and [helium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helium) (~25%), with much smaller quantities of heavier elements, including [oxygen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxygen), [carbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon), [neon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neon), and [iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron).
